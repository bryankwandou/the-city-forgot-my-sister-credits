Author's note

The City Forgot My Sister is a work of fiction. Kokage, Seisan Group, Kurojima Island, Kotobuki-za and every person in this book are invented. Osaka is real, and so are its back alleys, its fish harbour, its ramen and its takoyaki, and I hope I've been fair to it.

Craft influences

No text from any of these writers is quoted or copied in this book. They're the writers whose techniques I studied and tried to use. Listing them doesn't mean they've endorsed this book.

  • Suzanne Collins (The Hunger Games): first-person present-tense narration, and chapters that end on a jolt.
  • Dan Brown (The Da Vinci Code): short chapters, a clock that keeps running, and a hook at the end of every chapter.
  • Agatha Christie (And Then There Were None): fair-play clues planted early and paid off later. Here those are the shippu, the courier booking, the third glass and the light coffin.
  • Gillian Flynn (Gone Girl): a twist that changes the meaning of a detail the reader has already seen, like Ma's signature and Daisuke's own name in the log.
  • Blake Crouch (Dark Matter): one clean high concept, a fast pace, and a science-fiction climax that costs the hero something real.
  • Ted Chiang (Stories of Your Life and Others): a single rule of technology treated as an emotional question, with an ending built on the price of that rule.
  • Kazuo Ishiguro (The Remains of the Day, The Buried Giant): memory, forgetting, and what we owe the people we can't remember.
  • Andrea Hirata (Laskar Pelangi): warmth and humour inside hardship, in an Indonesian setting.
  • Stephen King (On Writing): plain sentences, concrete detail, and ordinary people under extraordinary pressure.
